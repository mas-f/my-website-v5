# Home surface brief

## Scope and mode

Public portfolio home for an Experience surface. The work itself must lead from the first viewport. Audience: recruiters, hiring managers, and prospective freelance clients evaluating Fikri Amrullah as a full-stack Laravel developer. Job: understand his focus quickly, inspect credible project work, then contact him or download the CV.

## Success

- A first-time visitor can describe the developer's focus and see evidence of full-stack work within one viewport.
- Project entries read as technical case files, not a gallery of equal cards.
- The page feels authored, calm, and precise, with a clear path to projects, contact, and CV.
- Placeholder project content is visibly provisional and does not claim real clients or outcomes.

## Constraints

- Laravel Blade, Tailwind CSS 4, vanilla JavaScript, MySQL.
- Indonesian copy with natural English technical terms.
- Light mode, off-white ground, charcoal ink, measured cobalt accent.
- No fabricated experience, clients, metrics, or testimonials.
- Must remain fast, accessible, responsive, and work on shared hosting.
- CV and LinkedIn are absent until the administrator supplies them.

## Chosen direction

**Build Log** — an engineer's field notebook and annotated project ledger. The page is a working sheet: graph-paper ground, graphite ink, cobalt annotations, manila index tabs, stamped statuses, and technical plates. The project index is a ledger of entries rather than a card grid. The memorable moment is the SDLC process map in the “Cara kerja” section, a technology-agnostic four-phase field diagram that closes with a feedback loop.

## Direction contract

THESIS: A developer portfolio as a working build log, refusing the category's centered hero, badge row, and equal-card project grid. The work is the page, and every claim is attached to a technical entry.

OWN-WORLD: Cool paper ground with a faint drafting grid; graphite ink; cobalt annotation ink; manila index tabs; thin rules; small stamped labels; soft paper-lift shadows. Type pairs a characterful grotesk display with a readable sans and a mono reserved for code, metadata, and measurements. Components look like ruled sheets, index tabs, margin notes, and ledger rows, not rounded SaaS cards.

STORY: The visitor sees a developer who thinks in systems, then reads four project entries that expose problem, solution, implementation, and trade-offs. They leave with a clear sense of capability and an obvious way to start a conversation.

FIRST VIEWPORT: One full-width drafting sheet. The name and a large statement occupy the upper-left field; a 4:5 portrait plate anchors the right field. A narrow left margin carries the current status and an index of the four entries. The primary action sits after the statement. The first project entry begins at the fold, not below a badge row.
